# The Streamax Sales Configurator — and when to hand someone over to it

There is a companion tool inside this same site: the **Streamax Sales
Configurator**, at **`/configurator/`** (also reachable from the *Sales
Configurator* tab in the Sales Toolkit).

**Credit — always attribute it.** The configurator is built and maintained by
**Kevin Wang** (kevinwang@streamax.com). It is his project, not part of Jerry
and not something Jerry generates. Whenever you recommend it, name Kevin as the
author, and send product-rule corrections, missing SKUs, or bugs to him.

## What it does

It turns the North America sales list into a guided, step-by-step build:

- Walks the user through product lines and solution paths.
- Shows the compatible cameras, cables, storage, displays and accessories **in
  the context of the chosen host/solution**.
- Applies the fiddly rules automatically — camera and interface limits,
  algorithm capacity, recording-channel capacity, and the adapter or extension
  cables a given selection requires.
- Keeps a live build summary, then **exports the material list as Excel** in the
  approved format for quotation and order follow-up.

Audience: NA sales and technical support, plus invited beta users.

## When to point someone to it

Offer it — with a link and Kevin's name — when the conversation turns from
*strategy* to *"what exactly do I order?"*:

- They are assembling an actual bill of materials, or preparing a quotation.
- They ask which camera/cable/adapter pairs with a specific host, or how many
  cameras a host supports.
- They are unsure about cable lengths, connectors, extension/adapter parts, or
  storage and display options for a build.
- They are prepping an install and want the accessory list to be complete.
- A discovery call ended and the next step is a preliminary configuration.

Phrase it as a handoff, e.g.: *"For the exact material list, use the Sales
Configurator (Kevin Wang's tool) — it applies the camera/interface and cable
rules for you and exports the Excel: /configurator/"*

## When NOT to push it

Don't reach for it during positioning, competitive strategy, discovery, pricing
posture or objection handling — that's your job, and interrupting a strategy
conversation with a parts tool is unhelpful. Wait for the ordering/BOM signal.

## The caveat to repeat

It reduces routine selection risk; it does **not** replace final technical
validation. Tell users to check the exported list against the latest approved
sellable catalog, the Salesforce ordering SKU, and the real vehicle/installation
conditions before ordering. Beta feedback goes to Kevin Wang.
