# Regulatory Calendar — the compliance clock, by market (verified Sept 2026)

**Why this file exists.** Regulation is Streamax's single best door-opener: it
creates a deadline the fleet cannot argue with. Jerry was repeatedly asked
region-strategy questions and had to answer "I don't know the local mandate,
check before you build a strategy" — most damagingly for **Japan**, where a
leadership user was explicitly blocked on it. That gap is closed below.

**Rule when using this file:** a mandate tells you *when the budget appears*.
It does not tell you who wins the deal. Lead with the date, win on the product.

---

## Japan — the mandate already happened. Sell the *retrofit* and the 3.5–8t band.

This corrects an open question in earlier Japan strategy discussions. Japan
has adopted the UNECE regulations (**R151, R158, R159**) and MLIT
(国土交通省) has *already mandated* side-collision warning
(側方衝突警報装置 — the R151 BSIS) for heavy trucks:

| Segment | Requirement | In force from |
|---|---|---|
| Trucks **over 8 t** — new type approvals | BSIS **mandatory** | 令和4年5月 — **May 2022** |
| Trucks **over 8 t** — continued production | BSIS **mandatory** | 令和6年5月 — **May 2024** |
| Trucks/buses **3.5 t – 8 t** | **Encouraged, not mandatory** — subsidy of **¥50,000** per installation | current |

Also: **NASVA** (National Agency for Automotive Safety & Victims' Aid) offers
tax reductions and installation subsidies for the >8t segment.

**What the system must do (R151):** below ~30 km/h, detect a cyclist or
similar VRU alongside the left flank and warn the driver — audible alert plus
in-cab lamp — when a left turn would risk a collision.

**Strategic reading — this changes the Japan plan:**

1. The ">8t new build" window is **closed**; OEM fitment is settled. Chasing
   it as a greenfield mandate play is chasing a bus that left in 2022.
2. The live opportunities are (a) the **retrofit/使用過程車 parc** of existing
   heavy trucks, and (b) the **3.5–8t band**, which is subsidised but not yet
   compelled — the classic pre-mandate window, and the subsidy means the buyer
   already has partial funding.
3. **BSIS/visibility is the correct product to lead with in Japan, not DMS.**
   It protects pedestrians and cyclists rather than watching the driver, which
   sidesteps the cultural resistance to in-cab driver monitoring. This was the
   right instinct in earlier strategy discussions and the regulation supports it.
4. R159 (**MOIS**, moving-off information) and R158 (reversing) are adopted
   too — a multi-regulation bundle sells better than a single-box sale.

## European Union — GSR2, and one date still in the future

| Requirement | Applies to | Date |
|---|---|---|
| BSIS, MOIS, reversing detection, ISA, AEB | **All new registrations** (cars, vans, trucks, buses) | **7 July 2024** — in force |
| Driver drowsiness/attention warning + enhanced AEB | New cars | **7 July 2026** — in force |
| **Improved direct vision for HGVs** | Heavy goods vehicles | **7 January 2029** — *still ahead* |

The 2029 direct-vision requirement is the one live EU deadline left to sell
against, and it is a **camera-and-visibility** requirement — squarely
Streamax's product. Start that pipeline now: 2029 sounds far away, but truck
purchase cycles and OEM design freezes are not.

## United Kingdom — DVS / PSS, already proven for us

London's Direct Vision Standard and its Progressive Safe System remain the
reference deployment: we have already won on this compliance-led motion, and
it is the template for Paris / Amsterdam / Brussels as DVS-equivalents spread.
Brigade Electronics' continental footprint is the distribution route.

## United States — no federal VRU camera mandate; the driver is insurance

There is no US federal equivalent of GSR2/R151. In NA the economic forcing
function is **insurance, litigation exposure and nuclear verdicts**, not a
compliance date. Sell exoneration and claims cost, not compliance.

## China-supplier restrictions — where the sanctions argument is real

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hikvision & Dahua on the **FCC Covered List** | New equipment authorizations banned since **Nov 2022** |
| **New escalation** | On **27 March 2026** the FCC proposed prohibiting *continued importation and marketing* of previously authorized Hikvision equipment — i.e. gear already approved is now in scope |
| Late 2025 | Enforcement push led to widespread retailer delistings |
| Scope | Public safety, government, critical infrastructure and national-security uses are the sharp end |

**Use it as a map, not a script.** The sanctions fact tells you *which
accounts are winnable* — US public-sector, government-adjacent and
critical-infrastructure fleets that cannot buy Hikvision/Dahua. It is not a
talking point to open with in a commercial fleet meeting; leading with a
competitor's legal problems reads as weakness and burns credibility.

⚠️ **Not verified:** whether **Brazil** (or other LatAm markets) has any
procurement restriction or legislative agenda on Chinese security equipment.
Searched, nothing found. **Do not assume there is one.** If a Brazilian
enterprise shipper has written supplier restrictions into carrier contracts,
that would be the strongest lever in that market — but it must be confirmed
